# How I work

How I believe we should work together

## How we build and ship

### Build what we know

Solve the problem we have now, not one we think we might have later. No speculative features, no "just in case" flexibility, no planning for a future we can't see yet. When we learn more, we build more.

### Purpose first, steer by the goal

Everything we build has a purpose. If we can't say why we're doing something, we don't do it. Every piece of work starts with a clear goal, and the goal decides what the right thing to build is, and in what order. When we're unsure what to do next, we go back to the goal and ask what moves us closest to it.

Everyone on the team knows the goal and understands why it matters. It's not something a few people keep in their heads. When everyone knows where we're going, anyone can make a good call without waiting for someone else to make it for them.

### Start small, ship small, keep going

Start with the smallest thing that makes a difference. Make each step something we could ship on its own. Keep going as long as the next step still matters. Stop when it doesn't, not when a deadline says so.

### Ship it, own it

Work that isn't launched isn't done. Ship early. When something is finished, it ships: not next week, not after one more round of polish, not hidden behind a flag "for now". What we start, we launch. Launching is like ripping off a band-aid: uncomfortable, and waiting only makes it worse. Real users teach us more in a day than a month of internal review.

Some things can be polished later. Others have to be right from the start: security, stability, and anything people depend on. We know the difference, and we never cut corners on what has to be right.

Shipping isn't the end. We follow what we launched, see how it lands, and pick up what breaks. We fix our own problems first, and we keep improving it after it's out.

### Leave it better than you found it

Improving what we touch is part of the work, not extra on top of it. Whether it's code, docs, designs, processes or a Linear board, we make time for it deliberately. If we skip it, we leave things worse for whoever comes next, and that is often us.

### Always move forward

Standing still is the worst outcome. It's better to try something, be wrong and learn than to wait for certainty and get nowhere. Make the call, see what happens, adjust.

Ideas come from anywhere. Anyone can question, suggest or help with anything, whatever their role. We decide together and respect each other's expertise. Most of the time the team just moves. Sometimes someone has to step up, and that someone can be anyone.

### Different views, one direction

We don't have to agree. Disagreement isn't about who is right or wrong. It's just different views, and it makes the work better. Once we decide, we move forward together and back each other, including the people who argued for something else. When things go wrong, nobody gets blamed, not one person and not a group. We work out what we learned and move on.

## How we treat each other

### Open by default

Talk in public channels. Everyone should be able to see everything and have the context. Private messages and closed groups are the rare exception, not the habit. If in doubt, post it where the team can see it.

### Honest, short and kind

Honesty comes before everything else, and you can be honest and still be kind. Say what you mean, keep it short, and make sure the people involved have the context they need.

### Ask, learn, teach

Reacting to each other's work is how we learn. Be curious and ask questions, because others can always teach you something. When you react, share what you know: suggest, guide, show another way. Every review and every comment is a chance to learn in both directions. And when something is good, say so, always, and in public. Praise is never implied.

### Always ask, always answer

Never hesitate to reach out. Don't guess whether someone is too busy or doesn't have time. Ask, call or ping, and let them decide. If they're in their focus bubble or just in the wrong headspace, they'll say so and book a time later. That is completely fine. No question is too small.

The other side of this is that no message goes unanswered and no thread is left behind. Someone always picks it up. Be available, and take the time to explain, share and help, especially for someone new or from another discipline. Silence, no reaction, no feedback: that's not how we work.

### Lighten the load

When someone is stuck or overloaded, we help without waiting to be asked. Move things around, take work off their plate, clear their calendar. Their problem is our problem.

### Room to be human

We are people before we are colleagues. Family comes first and work comes last. Kids, sickness and everything else outside work always win, and nobody has to explain or apologise for it. Everyone has bad days, and that's fine. We share both problems and joy, and we support each other when it's needed. Be open, have fun, and feel free to drift off topic and talk about anything. That is part of the work too.

## When everything feels urgent

Things will sometimes feel like they're on fire. Panic is contagious, and it spreads fast in a small team. Most things are not as important or as urgent as they seem at first.

So before we drop everything, we take a breath and step back. Ask:

- What actually happens if this waits an hour? A day? A week?
- Is this more important than what we're doing right now, or does it just feel louder?
- Does this need the whole team, or one or two people?

Most of the time the answer is "it can wait" or "one person can handle it", and the rest of us keep going. When something truly is critical, we act together and with focus, not in a rush. Calm people make better decisions.

Taking a moment to reflect is not standing still. It's how we make sure we move in the right direction.

## This will change

Change is easy, so we don't wait to make it. If we didn't do well today, we do better tomorrow. That goes for this too. Challenge it, and change it when we learn something new.
